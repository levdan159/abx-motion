ABX-Motion Rev 1.2 — integrated stepper research and implementation checklist
20 October 2026. Local development branch only. No hardware modified.

RECOMMENDATION

Retain one IC per axis and evaluate DRV8452SPWPR first, with DRV8461SPWPR
as the higher-voltage alternative. Do not freeze the earlier DRV8461 choice
before comparing its dissipation. Develop one checked axis circuit and real
placement envelope, then replicate three times. Maintain the Rev1.1d source,
manufacturing files and Git tag as the unmodified prototype baseline.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY REVIEWED

Manufacturer datasheets, motor-layout guidance, PowerPAD assembly guidance,
thermal metrics, the evaluation-board guide and extracted TI EVM schematic,

SOURCE-VERIFIED DEVICE DETAILS

DRV8461: SPI part DRV8461SPWPR; PWP0028T package. Operation4.5–65V;
3A full-scale/2.1A RMS subject to thermal limits. ENABLE low makes outputs
Hi-Z; sleep disables SPI. VCC determines SDO logic voltage. Internal-reference
selection bypasses external VREF, so a resistor divider is not an immutable
hardware current ceiling. [1, sections5,6,7.3.8,7.3.20]

Its local capacitor starting points are: VM–PGND10nF at each supply region;
CPH–CPL100nF VM-rated; VCP–VM1uF/16V; DVDD–GND1uF/6.3V;
VCC–GND100nF/6.3V; local VM bulk sized for the application. VCP–VM is a
floating capacitor, never a VCP-to-ground substitute. [1, table7-3, section11]

DRV8452: SPI part DRV8452SPWPR, also28-pin PowerPAD. Datasheet operation
4.5–55V; PWP capability4A full-scale/2.8A RMS is thermally conditional.
The25C PWP typical high/low resistances are57/60mOhm; maxima68/72mOhm.
At125C their maxima are100/105mOhm. Use this exact variant's reference
values and pin map; do not substitute its44-pin headline ratings. [2]

THERMAL COMPARISON — OUR CALCULATIONS, NOT MEASURED TEMPERATURES

For sinusoidal phase current, Pcond=2*Iphase_RMS^2*(R_HS+R_LS).
At2A RMS, using manufacturer tables specified at24V:

                     typical25C   maximum25C   maximum125C sensitivity
DRV8461 PWP, one IC      2.424W       2.920W           4.680W
DRV8452 PWP, one IC      0.936W       1.120W           1.640W
DRV8452, three ICs       2.808W       3.360W           4.920W

The resistance inputs for DRV8461 are153/150mOhm typical25C,
180/185mOhm maximum25C and285/300mOhm maximum125C. [1, section6.5]
Calculations exclude switching, quiescent, copper and connector losses.
They are comparative estimates at the datasheet test voltage, not guaranteed
32V bounds. The hot row does not predict the eventual junction temperature.
The lower-loss device reduces how much heat we need to remove before adding
heatsinks. calculate_losses.py reproduces18 cases and arithmetic assertions.

TI's thermal-metrics guide explains why JEDEC theta-JA is board/environment
dependent. It cannot certify our three adjacent drivers in an enclosure.
Use the actual stackup, copper coverage and neighboring heat sources when
estimating temperatures. Measure the first assembly at thermal equilibrium;
case temperature alone is not junction temperature. [5]

PLACEMENT AND ROUTING RULES TO APPLY

TI's motor-layout guide emphasizes short supply/return loops, local bypass
and charge-pump parts, wide current conductors and multiple plane-transition
vias. High-frequency capacitor loops should not share narrow returns with
logic/reference signals. Thermal vias should connect directly into planes;
thermal-relief spokes reduce heat transfer. Its generic guidance is a starting
point; the selected device/package takes precedence. [3]

Our proposed floorplan order is:
1. Place the three actual driver footprints and their grounded thermal regions.
2. Place charge-pump and smallest bypass parts before signal routing.
3. Place local bulk and motor connectors so switching loops remain local.
4. Route power and winding outputs, then logic/reference paths.
5. Keep an unbroken In1 ground plane and the original ESP32 antenna clearance.
6. Preserve unrelated DC-channel, encoder, communications and mechanical areas.

Use the present two-socket region for a real three-cell trial. Reserve copper
and capacitor volume as part of each cell; IC body dimensions alone do not
prove fit. There is no routed Rev1.2 placement yet. Prefer motor outputs on
outer copper with controlled loop areas; never expand them into giant heat
planes under the MCU/RF/ADC area. Match connector/contact capability to the
chosen phase-current objective and cable, rather than the battery fuse label.

THERMAL PAD, VIAS AND ASSEMBLY

PowerPAD guidance requires a soldered underside pad and efficient plane
connections. Via count has diminishing returns; hole size, pad coverage and
assembly quality matter together. Bottom-side-only tenting can increase
voiding through trapped gases, so 'tent everything' is not a universal rule.
The package drawing and assembler process must be reconciled. [4]

For our design: use the exact PWP0028T land/stencil drawings, then have a
single intentional thermal-via construction. Compare small vias coordinated
with paste openings against filled/capped via-in-pad. Include nearby
plane-stitching vias outside solder lands. Do not claim that setting KiCad's
via-tenting flag covers a hole inside an overlapping exposed-pad mask opening.
Review the actual mask/paste Gerbers. Do not drill arbitrary holes through
lead lands or copy Q1's electrically-live drain pattern into grounded IC pads.

JLCPCB distinguishes tented, plugged and filled/capped vias. These are different
fabrication choices; generic tenting does not guarantee a planar sealed
via-in-pad. If using solder-mask-defined thermal lands, call them out so CAM
does not enlarge their openings. Obtain assembly-process confirmation and
consider first-article X-ray of PowerPAD solder coverage. [9,10]

SUPPLY AND CAPACITOR DECISIONS

Keep the project7S/32V normal supply objective. A higher-rated driver does not
upgrade the buck, DC bridges, clamp, capacitors, Q1 or connectors. Retain the
existing protection envelope until a system-level change is explicitly checked.
Provide accessible VM/GND probe points at each cell. Budget shared input
current from electrical motor power and losses; phase RMS current is not
battery current and the three phase-current labels cannot simply be summed.

Select MLCCs using effective capacitance at operating bias and temperature,
not only their printed values; high-permittivity ceramic capacitance can fall
under DC bias. Check each actual supplier part's curves. [8]
For our32V bus, evaluate50/63V ceramic and bulk parts against measured
transients, ripple-current capability, ESR, temperature rating and lifetime.
Do not copy the EVM's bulk value as a universal solution. Use dV approximately
I*dt/C for a bounded transient estimate, then include ESR and cable inductance.

Returning current can raise the supply and switching-node voltage; TI's spike
mitigation guidance discusses local bulk and appropriate switching control. [11]
Our independent energy example:1000uF rising from32 to40V stores only0.288J
of additional energy. Bulk capacitance does not replace a return path for
sustained mechanical regeneration. Also check BMS charge acceptance, a full
pack and disconnects. Retain short-spike clamping; provide an external braking
solution only if the chosen mechanism needs it. Motor changes require power
off and discharged bus; arbitrary powered hot-plug robustness is not claimed.

LOGIC, SPI AND BOOT SAFETY

Proposed logic domain is3.3V: no5V MISO/nFAULT pull-up may reach an ESP32 pin.
Budget the additional logic load on U2 and validate3.3V droop during Wi-Fi and
motor activity. DVDD pins are local outputs; do not parallel the three driver
DVDD regulators or use them as a shared peripheral supply.

Keep all six STEP/DIR connections on direct ESP32 pins. Espressif documents
RMT hardware transmission and synchronization facilities; these can support
a pulse-engine implementation, but do not themselves provide trajectory
planning. DMA/memory availability and task/interrupt timing require review
against the actual ESP-IDF version. [12]
Our firmware work must cover synchronized acceleration/deceleration, pulse
width and DIR setup/hold, stop/abort, queue underflow and position accounting.
SPI handles setup/readback, not the real-time step train. Start with simple
fixed current/microsteps and verified faults; add automatic tuning afterward.

The PCA9555 powers up as inputs and has no external reset pin. Its POR does
not occur merely because the ESP32 restarts. [7]
Therefore our revision must establish safe enables and chip select with
external pulls and an independent hardware inhibit. Moving signals onto an
expander does not by itself solve reset safety. Initialize output latches to
safe values before making pins outputs. Test both cold boot and warm reset.
The current pin-allocation proposal still needs a dedicated hardware-arm/
reset/watchdog strategy; do not treat the five freed pins as a complete proof.

For daisy-chain configuration, review exact frame ordering and failure modes
of the whole chain. The draft expander-controlled chip select is conditional:
SPI and I2C operations need exclusive ownership, known inactive startup and
an abort path; keep outputs inhibited after a failed transaction. A direct
MCU chip select is preferable if the GPIO budget can accommodate it.

Keep per-axis enables and diagnostics. Implement the selected driver's logic
polarity explicitly; old StepStick active-low gating cannot be reused unchanged.
Keep outputs hardware-disabled while waking/configuring/readback occurs;
release only after valid settings and an explicit arm request. A motor must
not move because a status register or expander retained its previous value.
An E-stop should inhibit the bridge independently of either serial bus.
An MCU reset pin alone does not cover every firmware hang/software-reset case.

FAULTS, HOLD CURRENT AND LIMITS

TI's efficiency report documents motor/load-dependent current-reduction and
auto-torque tuning. Its examples demonstrate potential savings, not a universal
setup for arbitrary motors. [6]
Our policy: conservative startup current, deliberate hold-current choice,
latched application faults, explicit recovery and retained physical limits.
Reduce holding current only after checking required holding torque; disable
may release a vertical load. Sensorless stall detection is useful diagnostics,
not a replacement for hard stops or independent travel protection.

We currently have eight limit inputs. Reserve two more if both DC channels
and all three steppers each require min/max sensing. Do not silently trade
existing inputs away to make the GPIO or connector count appear complete.

EVM LESSONS AND CHECKPOINTS

The EVM guide provides individual current/microstep/fault controls and
jumper-selectable routing for different populated devices. Follow the selected
board's jumper population when comparing it with the circuit; debug headers,
USB tools and other-driver options need not be copied into our board. [13,14]
Our release gates, in order:
- Confirm exact driver orderable variant and JLCPCB availability/numbered pads.
- Close GPIO, logic-voltage, hardware inhibit and reset behavior on paper.
- Check one axis schematic against the chosen datasheet and reference circuit.
- Review three-cell placement, thermal copper, connectors and access.
- Route, then ERC/DRC/parity, pin-map and Gerber/CPL orientation checks.
- First article: rails/no unintended motion, one unloaded motor at low current,
  current waveform/VM ringing, then three-axis motion and worst holding heat.
- Repeat with Wi-Fi/CPU and intended DC-channel loading, enclosure and ambient.
- Exercise E-stop, reset, serial failure, brownout and deliberate fault recovery
  with bounded fault energy. Do not begin with destructive battery shorts.

actual motor/connector ratings;2oz outer copper feasibility with fine-pitch
routing; via fill/tenting and stencil process; full GPIO/hardware-arm allocation;
external-driver port retention; extra two limit inputs. The research identifies

files exist yet, and no powered test or component purchase was performed.
