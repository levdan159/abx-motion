ABX-Motion Rev 1.2 — layout integration plan, 21 October 2026

CURRENT AUTHORITY
This document supersedes the older DRV8452/DRV8461 and DDVR proposals for
the current placement. Selected driver is DRV8462DDWR, bottom exposed pad.
Three independent axes, 2 A RMS/phase normal target, occasional 3 A RMS
conditional on thermal validation. 7S battery; normal supply target to 32 V.
Passive heatsinks; no fan connector. No claim of powered/thermal validation.

The native root PCB is still a placement trial. All new footprints are
mechanical proxies, not electrically connected components. Zero reported
opens after removing old modules is NOT proof of a complete circuit: there
are no conductive pads on the new proxies. See schematic parity and the
placement audit before using any DRC summary.

KEEP THE APPROVED FLOORPLAN
Drivers U701/U702/U703: centres (88,124.5), (107,124.5), (126,124.5) mm,
all 0 degrees, 19 mm pitch. Power/output pins face left, controls face right.
Keep the repeated capacitor/pull arrangements. Keep U704 near the existing
I2C/digital-I/O side; U705..707 form the three candidate external interfaces;
U708..710 reserve inhibit/arming/supervision space. Existing DC, relay,
OLED/10 mm standoff, RF and communications arrangements stay fixed.
Do not add more unspecified logic 'just in case'. Replace package candidates
with the minimum verified circuit during schematic integration, and remove
any proxy the circuit does not need.

ROUTING FLOORPLAN — COARSE REGIONS, NOT ROUTED PATHS
1. Shared motor supply: evaluate a front-copper distribution band approximately
   X79.5..134.5, Y113..116 mm, entering from the protected motor bus on the
   right. Establish the actual branch point from the schematic/current copper.
   Give each driver its own low-impedance branch and local bulk loop.
   Do not chain the three power feeds through narrow IC pads or signal vias.
   This band is not a selected copper width or a qualified current path.
2. Phase outputs: group each driver's parallel output pins locally, then fan
   toward its motor connector below. Use front copper and In2 as needed;
   review the existing In2 zones before assigning any corridor to power.
   Do not force four winding nets through a single narrow side corridor.
3. Control: descend from the upper logic bank to the RIGHT side of each
   driver. Keep STEP/DIR and SPI away from switching-node bundles. Prefer
   a short configuration bus trunk with short branches. Decide chip-select
   architecture before laying out the bus. Route against a continuous ground
   reference and avoid plane-split crossings.
4. Ground/heat: retain continuous In1 GND. Keep the underside thermal-contact
   regions reserved at X82.5..93.5 / 101.5..112.5 / 120.5..131.5,
   Y115..132.5 mm. Join EP45, PGND and GND to low-impedance grounded copper
   and coordinated thermal-via arrays. Never connect a phase-output island
   to a common heatsink. Do not perforate the ground reference to make routing
   appear easier. Actual B.Cu copper, mask, TIM insulation and heatsink fixing
   are not designed yet.
5. Retained obstacles: encoder, button, I/O and supply traces already cross
   the bay. Respect them. Only identify obsolete StepStick routing for later
   selective retirement. No board-wide collision rip-up or legacy rebuild.

LOCAL DDWR PIN GROUPS — TOP VIEW, VERIFY AGAIN IN THE SCHEMATIC
 VCP=1; VM=2,11,12,21; PGNDA=3,10; PGNDB=13,20.
 AOUT1=4,5,6; AOUT2=7,8,9; BOUT2=14,15,16; BOUT1=17,18,19.
 GND=22,23 and exposed pad 45 in the footprint.
 DVDD=24; VCC=25; nFAULT=26; nHOME=27; MODE=28.
 RSVD=29,30,31,32 and (in SPI mode)35: leave unconnected per datasheet.
 VREF=33; nSCS=34; SDO=36; SDI=37; SCLK=38; STEP=39; DIR=40.
 ENABLE=41; nSLEEP=42; CPL=43; CPH=44.
 DDVR is a different pinout and is not a drop-in alternate.

Two local 10 nF VM-rated capacitors go to VM/PGND supply pairs. Keep the
100 nF VM-rated charge-pump capacitor at CPH/CPL, and the 1 uF/16 V capacitor
between VCP and VM, not VCP and ground. Keep 100 nF at VCC and 1 uF at
DVDD. These are datasheet starting points; choose actual procurement parts
and verify voltage/bias/ripple. Never parallel the three DVDD regulator outputs.
VCC/logic-domain selection must protect the ESP32 from 5 V SDO/pullups.

0.635 mm lead pitch and 0.4 mm lands leave only 0.235 mm between leads.
At our 0.15 mm signal/clearance minimum, a between-lead trace would need
0.45 mm. Escape outward. Stagger transition vias around capacitors/pulls.
Do not select thermal-via construction or insert holes into the exposed pad
until the stencil/mask and assembly process are reviewed together.

CONNECTOR AND MECHANICAL PLAN
J711/712/713 use the original upright JST-XH B4B-XH-A four-position,
2.5 mm pitch footprint, centred on each axis at Y148 mm. Matching cable
housing: XHP-4 with correctly selected genuine crimp contacts.
Suggested pin mapping for review: 1=A1, 2=A2, 3=B1, 4=B2; all four pins
are in one row. Verify the original harness convention before assigning nets.
JST specifies 3 A with AWG22 and a temperature range including self-heating.
Normal 2 A RMS operation still requires wire/contact/temperature qualification;
3 A RMS has no nameplate current margin and is not a qualified operating
mode merely because the driver or heatsink can support it. Do not silently
raise the connector limit to match the IC. Check mating access and wire bend.

C717/727/737 use D8/P3.5 radial packages as fit candidates only. Select bulk
capacitance, voltage, ripple-current and lifetime first. If an adequate part
needs D10 or more height, revise placement before routing. A larger voltage
rating does not establish ripple or regenerative-energy capability.

J701/702/703 are upright external-control OUTPUT candidates. They preserve
ESP32 control when an external driver is used. Four pins are provisional:
do not freeze pin order, voltage, sink/source polarity or buffering until
the supported external-driver interface is defined. They are not motor ports.
JP701/702/703 reserve per-axis onboard/external mode selection. The hardware
mode must prevent accidental onboard bridge drive; it must not merely change
the firmware label. Heatsink and cable access must remain usable in both modes.

ELECTRICAL WORK THAT MUST PRECEDE ROUTING
A. Export the actual native schematic netlist and close the ESP32 pin budget.
   Six STEP/DIR signals require direct MCU pins. Existing GPIO11..14 serve
   two axes; the older proposal moves UI/relay controls to a second expander
   to free pins, but the third axis, SPI, chip-select and hardware arm still
   need a checked allocation. Do not borrow strap/input-only pins casually.
B. Define the minimum hardware truth table: E-stop, MCU reset, watchdog loss,
   missing 3V3, expander power-up, failed SPI configuration, per-axis mode,
   per-axis enable and driver fault. Outputs must remain inhibited until
   valid configuration and an explicit arm action. Inhibit must work without
   a functioning I2C/SPI bus. Expander retained state is not a reset strategy.
C. Choose exact gate/buffer/supervisor devices to implement that truth table.
   U705..710 are package envelopes, not selected ICs. Confirm their pinouts,
   package size, default outputs, supply sequencing and required passives.
   A supervisory-reset device alone is not automatically a firmware watchdog.
D. Give each driver local fault observation and configuration readback.
   Avoid implicit motion restart after a fault or reset. Define what each
   external output does when inhibited, in either mode, and with power absent.
E. Implement one complete driver cell in the schematic, review it, then copy
   to Y/Z. Include all required pulls/straps and power pins, reserved-pin
   treatment, reference choice and current settings. An internal VREF option
   can bypass an external divider, so a divider alone is not a hardware limit.
F. Replace proxies with real schematic-linked footprints and check parity.
   Delete unnecessary provisional parts at this point. Do not manufacture
   from the placement-only footprint, which lacks approved EP paste/vias.

ROUTING ORDER AFTER ELECTRICAL REVIEW
1. Freeze actual connector/capacitor/support-device footprints and pin maps.
2. Identify obsolete track UUIDs by net plus connection/physical scope; preserve
   shared supply/ground and all unrelated routing. Save a rollback snapshot.
3. Route one axis's power loops, four winding nets and thermal connections.
   Review width, copper weight, temperature rise, neck-downs, vias, supply
   ripple and return paths before repeating. Phase RMS is not battery current.
4. Replicate the reviewed geometry, adapting to real obstacles on each axis.
5. Route hardware inhibit/faults, then STEP/DIR, SPI/I2C and slow status nets.
6. Review thermal-contact copper and all power/return paths; refill zones;
   check ERC, full DRC and schematic parity. Fix the causes of errors rather
   than relaxing constraints or repeatedly ripping up the whole board.
7. Stop for review before fabrication/assembly exports. Routing and DRC do
   not establish three-axis thermal performance or 3 A RMS capability.
