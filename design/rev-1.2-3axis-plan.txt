ABX-Motion Rev 1.2 — three integrated stepper axes
Discussion draft, 20 October 2026

BASELINE AND STATUS

Start from commit12ad71f6982117bd97d409770fdfee5e75b0d575, tag rev-1.1d,
on branch rev-1.2-3axis-stepper. Rev1.1d source and manufacturing package
remain unchanged. No Rev1.2 schematic/layout/firmware has been implemented.
The files in production/rev-1.1d-20261020 are still the current matched
prototype candidate, not a three-axis manufacturing release.

INTENT

Retain157x105mm board outline, mounting holes, ESP32, two DC channels,
CAN/RS485, OLED/UI, auxiliary outputs and relays. Replace the two StepStick
socket assemblies with three independently controlled integrated stepper
circuits. Reuse the current stepper region rather than insert a third module
into remaining gaps. Driver selection and achievable current remain proposals.

RESEARCH UPDATE — 20 October 2026

The documentation review now favors evaluating DRV8452SPWPR first for its
lower package-specific losses at our 32 V target; DRV8461SPWPR remains the
higher-voltage alternative. This supersedes the initial driver preference
below, not the retained one-IC-per-axis architecture. Neither part is frozen
in a schematic. See rev-1.2-research/integration-review.txt and the reproducible
conduction-loss-comparison.csv. GPIO/hardware arm and the assembly thermal-via
process remain open design items. No Rev 1.2 hardware changes have been made.

INITIAL DRIVER PROPOSAL (historical starting point)

Evaluate three TI DRV8461SPWPR (SPI version,28-pin PowerPAD HTSSOP), one per
motor. Verify exact orderable part, current JLCPCB/LCSC sourcing and supplier
land pattern before committing the schematic. The 65V operating range is
useful for our7S/32V target; it does not raise the whole board voltage rating.
Integrated sensing avoids external phase shunts. Direct STEP/DIR handles
motion; SPI handles settings and diagnostic reads. The supported daisy chain
can reduce chip-select resources. Distinguish the SPI variant from the H/W
variant, whose same-size package has a different interface pin assignment.

Provisional board objective: approximately2A RMS phase current per axis,
subject to simultaneous-axis PCB/enclosure thermal verification. Begin bench
commissioning substantially lower. Manufacturer full-scale3A is approximately
2.1A RMS for sinusoidal excitation, not a promise of3A RMS continuously.
If higher phase current is a core requirement, compare DRV8452/DRV8462 with
their exact package/thermal limits before layout. TMC2240 offers useful quiet
motion/telemetry, but its36V range makes transient protection coordination
more demanding. Do not select solely from headline peak-current ratings.

GPIO AND CONTROL

Keep all six STEP/DIR signals on ESP32 GPIOs, driven by a coordinated hardware
pulse engine. I2C expanders must never generate the step pulse train.
The current module GPIO budget is effectively full. Add a second PCA9555 at
a verified unused address, provisionally0x21; existing device is0x20. Move
three navigation buttons and both relay controls to slow I/O, freeing five
ordinary MCU GPIOs. A provisional allocation is existing GPIO11..14 for
axes1/2 STEP/DIR, freed GPIO47/48 for axis3 STEP/DIR, and freed GPIO21/37/40
for SPI SCLK/MOSI/MISO. SPI daisy-chain chip select may use expander GPIO
because configuration transactions are not the step timing source; serialize
SPI with chip-select writes, default it inactive and verify startup behavior.
This allocation is a proposal requiring schematic/boot/firmware review.
Do not depend on changing boot-strapping pins or changing the N8R2 module SKU.

Use independent per-axis enables, fault indication and hardware E-stop
inhibition with default-disabled pulls. GPIO-expander reset persistence must
not permit motion after a warm MCU reset. Establish known current/microstep
settings and read back configuration before arming. Driver faults should
stop the relevant commanded motion and latch the application fault; hardware
auto-retry is not permission to resume. Hardware E-stop must work without
I2C, SPI or application firmware. Disable/HiZ may remove holding torque;
a falling-load axis needs a separately considered mechanical restraint.

THERMAL, POWER AND LAYOUT

Place three repeated driver cells in the region freed by sockets, switches
and associated components. Each cell includes local ceramic supply bypass,
charge-pump parts, logic decoupling, local bulk capacitance, reference/current
setting, driver ground/thermal pad, fault/enable circuitry and motor connector.
Choose component values from the exact datasheet/application circuit.

Give every grounded PowerPAD a short, low-impedance connection to the internal
ground plane and a local via array/backside spreader. Coordinate thermal-hole
and stencil design with JLCPCB; do not put arbitrary open holes in paste
apertures. Preserve the solid In1 ground plane and ESP32 antenna keepout.
Separate high-current loops from ADC/encoder/RF signals. Allow airflow and
optional insulated chassis coupling; route no new switching-node heat plane
under the ESP32 or its antenna.

Recalculate simultaneous DC+stepper input currents, Q1 dissipation, shared
copper/via current paths, fuse coordination, terminal/contact ratings, bulk
capacitor ripple and battery/BMS energy acceptance. Holding torque also causes
stepper heat without shaft motion. Enclosure and ambient temperature matter;
more PCB copper cannot automatically guarantee better cooling than a module.
Existing D2 remains a short-pulse clamp, not a braking-energy dump load.

CONNECTORS AND LIMITS

Provide three dedicated4-wire motor connectors near their driver circuits.
Try the existing four-position bottom connector region first. Decide how
many external STEP/DIR interfaces to retain before allocating that edge;
retain useful flexibility if placement allows, rather than promise all ports.
Eight existing limit inputs cover three dual-limit steppers plus two single
DC limits. Ten inputs are needed if all five motors require separate minimum
and maximum signals; reserve two new expander inputs and an additional
protected3-pin limit header if that is required. Preserve the3.3V/dry-contact
input contract; these are not24V industrial inputs.

IMPLEMENTATION CHECKPOINTS

1. Agree current/driver/interface priorities; check sourcing and exact variant.
2. Prove GPIO allocation, default-disarmed logic, faults and E-stop behavior.
3. Create a non-routed placement trial with real footprints, passives,
   connectors, thermal regions and service access. Review fit before routing.
4. Implement one fully checked stepper schematic cell, then replicate it.
5. Route locally, preserving unrelated circuitry; review power/return paths.
6. Run ERC/DRC/parity, pin-map and supplier-placement checks. Export a separate
   Rev1.2 Gerber/BOM/CPL package only after the native design is complete.
7. Prototype staged one-axis, then three-axis, holding-current and simultaneous
   DC/stepper thermal tests, reset/fault/E-stop tests and intended motor tests.
