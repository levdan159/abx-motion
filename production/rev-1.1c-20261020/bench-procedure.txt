ABX-Motion Rev 1.1c — low-risk bench acceptance at the intended battery voltage
20 October 2026

STATUS: PROCEDURE PREPARED; NO POWERED BOARD TESTS HAVE BEEN RUN.
Use the matching Rev 1.1c Gerbers/BOM/CPL. Resolve the relay placement exception
before assembly. Static review does not certify zero failures on a battery.

OBJECTIVE

Check two DC channels for 150 W electrical input per motor for one second,
then simultaneous 300 W for one second. This excludes StepStick/peripheral
loads and is an intermittent edge case. Use measured bus electrical input
power for this test; motor-nameplate mechanical watts are different.
Keep D2 as the specified short-spike clamp. Do not force it to absorb a
one-second braking event. No deliberate output short, battery reversal or
power-limit destruction test is part of this procedure.

PREPARATION

Use the intended 7S battery voltage, measured before connecting it, or an
energy-limited source at that same voltage. There is no 12 V bring-up
requirement. Normal bus voltage must remain <=32 V; 7S full charge is 29.4 V.
Use correct-polarity keyed wiring and a suitable inline fuse close to the
battery. F1 is a fuse HOLDER: the actual fuse must be fitted separately.
The printed 25 A target is not a safe starting test current or a precision
current limit. A fuse can allow damaging short pulses before it clears.

Have a current-limited test fixture, bus-current measurement, oscilloscope
and temperature measurement available. For a battery-only first power,
100 ohm / 25 W in series, with motors/carriers/other loads disconnected and
the MCU held in RESET, is a simple precharge/fault-energy check. At 32 V it
limits a hard short to approximately 0.32 A (about 0.337 A with -5% resistance);
resistor dissipation under that short is approximately 10–11 W. Mount the
resistor so its heat cannot damage wiring. It is NOT a motor-running supply
or a regenerative brake. If the board does not charge normally, disconnect
and inspect; do not bypass the resistor to force it to start. A proper
current-limited fixture may be used instead. Avoid repeated live hot-plugging.

A source that limits outgoing current may NOT accept returning motor energy.
Do not substitute an ordinary source-only bench supply, e-fuse or blocking
adapter for a verified regenerative-energy path during powered stopping tests.
No motor test progresses to high speed until that path is established.

Power off before wiring changes; open J102 E-stop and disconnect motors,
StepStick carriers, external drivers, relays' loads and AUX loads initially.
Inspect the actual populated parts against the new BOM, especially R302/R402
12k, D2 pin 1 lower-right, Q1, diode/electrolytic polarity and socket strips.
Do not plug A4988/TMC2209 or arbitrary carrier types into the 32 V system.
DRV8825-compatible pinout and voltage suitability must both be established;
set the actual carrier's own current limit separately.

MEASUREMENT RULES

Measure VBAT at the bridge bulk capacitors and driver VM, not only at the
battery connector. Probe gate-to-SOURCE voltage; use a differential or other
suitably isolated probe for high-side gates and motor phase nodes. Do not
attach an ordinary grounded scope clip to a switching phase/high-side node.
Use a phase-current probe or suitable isolated shunt measurement for winding
current. Input/battery current and winding current differ during PWM.
DRV8701 SO measures driving shunt current; it does NOT measure recirculating
brake current. SO and an inline power meter serve different purposes.

INITIAL POWER — NO MOTORS

1. Unpowered: confirm polarity, fuse continuity and absence of an obvious
   supply short. Resistance readings must be allowed to settle as caps charge.
   Verify assembly connections, especially the relay pin 1/coil-row geometry.
2. Battery precharge/current-limited first power at the intended voltage with
   RESET held and E-stop open. Confirm VBAT rises normally and no unexpected
   current or heating occurs. Remove RESET only after those checks pass.
3. Confirm 5 V within 4.85–5.20 V and 3.3 V within 3.20–3.40 V as conservative
   commissioning targets; investigate cycling, overshoot or radio brownouts.
   These are test acceptance targets, not replacement component specifications.
   Complete the no-motor checks before bypassing the precharge resistor.
4. In isolation test USB-only, battery-only, then correct-polarity dual power
   through the energy-limited fixture. Measure whether USB backfeeds VBAT
   through the buck; do not assume reverse-polarity protection covers an
   externally backpowered bus. Do not deliberately reverse a high-current pack.
5. Confirm expander 0x20, input states and AUX off. Configure outputs with
   safe latch values BEFORE changing direction registers. Check MCU-only
   reset as well as cold boot: the expander retains state through MCU reset.
6. With all motor commands inactive, close/open J102. Opening must bring
   DRV nSLEEP below 0.8 V and StepStick EN to disabled. Observe actual gate
   shutdown timing. A 1 ms response is a commissioning ceiling, not a claimed
   datasheet guaranteed limit. Relays/AUX remain powered by design.

CONTROL PREREQUISITES BEFORE MOTOR POWER

There is no tested application firmware in this repository. Do not run a
simple PWM sweep as though it provides current, energy or timeout protection.
Require boot-disarmed commands, a <=1 second bounded burst timer, conservative
PWM ramp, calibrated ADC scaling, encoder/command sanity checks, and a latched
fault state requiring an explicit re-arm. Set EN low on reset/fault and prevent
new drive commands. On DRV8701E, EN low applies low-side BRAKING, not high-Z.
Opening J102/nSLEEP is the hardware high-Z disable path.

The driver automatically retries OCP/gate faults after about 3 ms. EN-low
software alone cannot guarantee that every wiring fault is isolated because
low-side brake FETs can still conduct. For a persistent fault, keep hardware
E-stop open and disconnect power; do not allow repeated fault/restart cycles.
The driver's thermal shutdown measures its own die, not Q1 or external FETs.
Confirm the fault latch using low-energy logic fault injection first; that
does not prove the bridge can survive a real battery output short.

BOUNDED MOTOR TESTS

7. Measure/record winding resistance and inductance where possible, secure one
   unloaded motor, and use a low-current test setting/fixture at the intended
   voltage. Start with 100 ms low-PWM commands. No instant direction reversal.
   Observe current rise, both gate waveforms, VM/phase ringing and nFAULT.
8. Confirm actual chopping current against the new 9.35 A nominal target using
   an appropriate controlled load and energy-limited setup, NOT an abrupt
   locked-rotor battery test. The static model estimate is 6.7–11.8 A and
   excludes resistor temperature drift, PCB ground errors and current overshoot
   during 2 us blanking/comparator/turn-off delay. Very low-inductance motors
   can exceed it substantially. Measure; do not treat 9.35 A as a hard ceiling.
9. Test each channel separately with a controlled mechanical/electrical load:
   low load first, then approximately 25 W/250 ms,50 W/250 ms,100 W/500 ms,
   finally 150 W/1 s. Stop after every step to inspect waveforms and temperatures.
   Repeat only after components return within 5 C of their starting temperature;
   allow at least 60 seconds between final-power test bursts initially.
10. Once both channels independently pass, repeat the short progression with
    both channels, up to 150 W each/1 s. Measure total input power/current;
    at 24 V,300 W corresponds to 12.5 A before controller losses. Q1's cold
    resistive loss at 12.5 A is about 2.81 W; this is not a temperature rating.
    Use an appropriately sized fuse/fixture after lower-level tests pass;
    do not interpret a25 A fuse as permission for 25 A continuous PCB current.
11. Check low-energy stops first, then increase speed gradually while measuring
    signed battery current and bus voltage. Coast, EN-low brake and direction
    reversal have different behavior. Do not assume all consumed energy must
    return to the bus, or assume the BMS will accept it at full charge.
    Forced backdrive, a falling load or BMS disconnect can require an external
    brake. A two-channel 150 W/1 s RETURNED-energy case is 300 J; the local
    capacitors/TVS are not a qualified 300 J dump load. Do not test that case
    until an energy sink with adequate pulse/repetition capability is installed.

STOP / INVESTIGATE CRITERIA

- Unexpected command, nFAULT assertion, repeated restart, wrong gate sequence,
  shoot-through signature, unstable current, rail cycling or missed timeout.
- VM reaching 40 V or above during commissioning, or any sustained bus rise
  beyond 32 V: stop progressing and investigate.40 V is the short-pulse TVS
  clamp specification, not a normal operating target or a guaranteed board
  waveform. Use suitable hardware energy containment; software reaction alone
  cannot protect against every fast overvoltage event.
- Gate-to-source magnitude approaching 15 V, unexpected negative ringing,
  or phase excursion approaching the DRV8701 absolute pin limits. Verify
  probe referencing before interpreting these readings. Gate absolute rating
  is 20 V; do not use the absolute maximum as an acceptance target.
- During initial qualification, any power component case approaching 70 C,
  rapid unexplained heating, increasing voltage drop or drifting readings.
  Case temperature does not establish junction temperature; use a conservative
  stop threshold and investigate before repeating bursts.
