ABX-Motion Rev 1.1c — final design robustness review
20 October 2026

RESULT

The design checks pass after a small current-limit reduction. This is NOT a
powered robustness-test pass: no physical motor, stall, short, thermal or
regeneration test was performed. The saved CAD is a prototype fabrication
candidate; physical qualification requires the first boards. Confirm relay
alignment with the assembler before assembly. Full-power operation and the
return-energy/protection envelope remain unqualified until bench testing.
The matching candidate is production/rev-1.1c-20261020/.

D2 COPPER AREA

The broad solder-mask-covered copper beside D2 is retained from the former
SMC diode layout and connected to the local power/ground paths. It is not
an enlarged exposed solder land or stencil aperture. The new package has
its own matching contacts and paste openings. Retain this copper; cosmetic
shrinking is unnecessary and does not improve the clamp energy rating.

ACTUAL FIX

R302/R402 change from 30k / C22984 to 12k / C22790 (UNI-ROYAL 0603WAF1202T5E,
12k,1%,100mW,75V,0603). R301/R401 remain 18k; shunts remain 10 mΩ/3W.
VREF becomes nominal 1.92V; with gain 20 and 50mV typical offset, current
chopping is 9.35A nominal instead of 14.75A. The current loop remains the
existing DRV8701 hardware loop; ADC scaling and topology are unchanged.
No components were moved and no tracks, vias, holes or pads were changed.
Revision text/title blocks and the rear chop-current legend are updated to
Rev 1.1c /9.4A to identify the changed build. No second TVS was added.

WHY THIS HELPS

For the user's two 150W electrical/one-second motor peaks, combined input is
300W: approximately 12.5A at 24V,10.20A at 29.4V,9.375A at 32V before controller
losses. These are bus currents. Motor winding current may be higher under PWM.
The old 15A nominal chopping setting allowed considerable extra startup/stall
stress. Reducing the hardware threshold lowers that stress and shunt heating.
This is not a150W power limiter and may reduce available torque for a low-
voltage motor operated through PWM. It does not establish continuous ratings.

ANALYTICAL CORNERS — ESTIMATES, NOT GUARANTEED MOTOR-CURRENT LIMITS

64 parameter combinations per resistor selection (128 total): AVDD 4.4–5.2V,
gain 18–22, divider/shunt initial tolerances 1%, offset model 0–250mV.
TI specifies offset 50mV typical/250mV maximum without a minimum; zero is a
modelling floor here. Do not treat this sweep as a guaranteed device bound.

                     Nominal I    Static-model range    Max shunt power
Old 18k/30k           14.75A        11.16–18.37A           3.342W
New 18k/12k            9.35A         6.70–11.81A           1.381W

The old model can exceed the shunt's nominal 3W rating; the new model provides
more margin. This excludes resistor temperature drift, ground/Kelvin errors,
current-ramp overshoot and thermal derating. The PCB sense return remains a
shared ground connection, not a newly implemented dedicated Kelvin pair.
DRV8701 ignores current sense during 2us blanking. At 32V, an ideal no-back-EMF
ramp adds about 0.64A over 2us with 100uH, but 6.4A with 10uH. Extra comparator/
switching delay adds further overshoot. Unknown low-inductance motors and
output shorts are therefore not made safe merely by choosing 12k resistors.

At 12.5A total input, Q1 dissipates about 2.81W using 18 mΩ cold resistance;
a2x-resistance sensitivity case gives 5.63W. These are loss calculations,
not a predicted safe junction temperature. A bridge's two-FET cold conduction
loss at 6.25A is about 0.258W using 3.3 mΩ per FET; PWM winding RMS current,
switching loss, temperature and actual copper cooling still matter.
The nominal 25mA gate-current setting charges the MOSFET's53nC maximum
10V gate charge in about 2.12us as a simple estimate. Actual gate transitions
and ringing must be observed; this is not a transistor switching simulation.

FAILURE-MODE REVIEW

Normal 32V supply: component voltage selections are consistent with the normal
ceiling. The specified 40V short-pulse clamp is below the DRV8701's45V operating
ceiling / 47 V absolute maximum and below 50V bulk/60V power FET ratings. Actual
local overshoot is unmeasured. Do not infer protection for arbitrary pulses.

Startup/stall: lower hardware chopping is implemented. Safe for unrestricted
stall or prolonged repeated peaks is NOT established. Firmware must impose
bounded commands and measured power/thermal limits; no such application
firmware was implemented or tested here.

Output short/gate fault: DRV8701 has OCP/predriver fault protection, but it
retries after approximately 3ms. A fuse and the driver's own thermal shutdown
are not guaranteed protection for Q1/external FETs during every battery short.
No battery-powered intentional-short test is authorized by this procedure.

Brake/stop: DRV8701E EN low turns on the low-side brake path; it is NOT a
high-impedance disconnect. SO does not measure brake recirculating current.
Opening J102 pulls nSLEEP low and provides the high-Z hardware disable path.
A normal coast does not necessarily dump all motor energy into the bus;
forced backdrive, reversal and a disconnected/non-accepting BMS need separate
attention. The existing hardware stop does not cut relay/AUX power.

Returned-energy case: if the motor specification also requires 150W returned
per channel for 1s, that is 300J. The nominal 640uF motor bulk capacitors can
store only 0.235J additional energy from 29.4 to 40V, or 0.184J from 32 to 40V.
TVS3300 is specified for short surges, not a300J/one-second dump. A verified
battery return path or sized external brake is required for that separate
case. Two unequal TVS devices in direct parallel do not establish sharing.

Hot-plug/dual power: cable inductance and inrush can create local ringing.
Use precharge/limited fault energy for first power at the intended voltage.
USB-only buck backfeed and reverse-polarity behavior with another source
connected have not been characterized on hardware; do not deliberately
reverse a high-current battery to find out. Validate normal USB-only,
battery-only and correct-polarity dual-power behavior first.

Reset/fault handling: EN has external pulldowns, fault lines have 3.3V pullups,
and the expander defaults/net routing were checked. MCU warm reset does not
reset the expander. Existing PCA output states can persist. A firmware fault
latch is not interchangeable with the driver's automatic retry mechanism.

Peripherals: this 150W case covers the two brushed DC channels. StepStick
supply/current limits belong to the actual carrier; TMC2209 is unsuitable
for 7S full charge. Limit inputs remain dry-contact/3.3V-only. No universal
motor, current, mains or industrial input qualification is implied.
