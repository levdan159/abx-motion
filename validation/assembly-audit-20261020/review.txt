agreement. Current public supplier footprint SVGs were obtained for all 66
SKUs through links on LCSC product pages. The blocked EasyEDA JSON endpoint
is no longer the source of the earlier D2/U601/PH orientation uncertainty.
Raw SVGs and reproducible read-only audit code are archived alongside this
report. The conversion is one consistent transform: supplier coordinates

Supplier pad centers land on corresponding PCB copper for 237 placements.
The two relay placements have matching rotation but an inconsistent supplier

single same-net drain pad 5. USB combined supplier pin names and four shell
legs map to the corresponding native pins and S1. Unpolarized fuse-holder
and socket-strip pin renumbering is explicitly handled. This is a geometry
and numbering audit; it is not a claim that every supplier CAD pin label is
infallible or that an actual assembly order has been reviewed or approved.

RELAY EXCEPTION

K501/K502 use Omron G5LE-14 DC5, C116963. Omron specifies 2.00 mm between
common pin1 and the coil-pin row; PCB matches that manufacturer drawing.
Supplier preview instead has 2.55 mm. Current supplier-to-PCB residual is
about 0.440 mm for common pin1, and 0.110 mm for the other four pins. Rotation
270 is consistent with coil/contact placement, but this malformed CAD cannot
certify the physical insertion origin. Do not change PCB holes to fit it.
Have the assembler align the real relay to the official Omron hole drawing:
K501: 1=(146,122), 2=(140,124), 3=(140,136.2), 4=(152,136.2), 5=(152,124) mm.
K502: same pattern translated +19 mm in X.
Hole diameter is 1.3 mm. Read drawings as bottom view where specified; the
coordinates above are PCB top view. Coil is pins2/5; COM1, NO3, NC4.

D2 CLOSE-UP / SOLDERING

The small package on the large patch is intentional: D2 is a 2x2 mm WSON,
replacing an SMC diode. Existing supply copper was preserved under solder
mask. Fresh fabrication outputs match all 13 files in the prior package
geometrically, excluding generation timestamps. D2's F.Mask section contains
exactly the six new small lead lands and the EP opening; F.Paste contains
six lead apertures plus two reduced EP apertures. The old SMC solder lands
are absent. Large underlying copper is not a large stencil opening and does
not create a solder bridge. The package's size alone does not establish its
surge-energy suitability.

PROTECTION DECISION — DO NOT ORDER THE CURRENT CANDIDATE YET

TVS3300DRVR: 33 V standoff; up to 40 V clamp for TI's specified 8/20 us pulse;
35 A at 8/20 us, but only 3.5 A / 125 W at 10/1000 us in the SON package.
Old SMCJ30A family: 30 V standoff; nominal 1500 W at 10/1000 us; maximum
specified clamp 48.4 V at its rated current. That exceeds DRV8701's 47 V
absolute maximum, and 30 V standoff does not cover the requested normal 32 V.
Neither option establishes unrestricted regenerative-energy protection.

The user offered room for both. Populating two unequal clamps directly in
parallel does not guarantee useful current/energy sharing: the lower-clamp
part can absorb most of the event. TI explicitly cautions against assuming
parallel ratings add. No second diode or unvalidated sharing network was
added. The earlier replacement is therefore a candidate requiring a deliberate
protection decision, not a released robustness fix for unknown motor loads.

Retaining a large-TVS footprint as a build/rework option is possible, but
that is distinct from approving both parts populated. A coordinated clamp
needs a defined surge current/duration and supply impedance. Sustained motor
energy must return to a battery/BMS able to accept it or a sized braking path;
adding a second surge diode does not qualify that behavior. The >=30 A pack
rating describes source capability, not an allowed TVS or PCB load current.
Keep the existing successful layout while resolving the local protection
choice. No claim of 32 V/full-current or arbitrary-peripheral qualification
is made by these static checks.
