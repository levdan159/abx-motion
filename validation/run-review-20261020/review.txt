WHAT HAPPENED DURING THE RUN

The earlier broad rip-up run removed unrelated legal tracks, including USB,
power and motor circuitry. Its completed candidate had 141 copper/mask errors
and 45 missing connections. Those changes are absent from the current saved
board because the compact recovery checkpoint was restored.

Two later repair helpers remain in the project:
- apply_localized_repairs.py
- test_astar_5.py

Both copy /tmp/checkpoint_base.kicad_pcb over the working PCB at startup. The
A* helper then performs repairs in memory and saves only after its searches
finish. Cancelling during search can therefore leave the original checkpoint
on disk and discard the in-memory partial progress. The late background save
confirmed that more than one writer was still active. Neither helper provides
a safe incremental repair history.

The newer A* checker does improve on the older router: it checks other-net
clearance at endpoints, rejects endpoints outside the search box, and attempts
to enforce same-net drill spacing. However, it still uses approximate pad
rectangles and node/midpoint checks, does not account for all artwork/keepouts
and zone geometry, and scans large pad/track lists while searching. Its main
search loop has no runtime or expansion cap. This explains why it can spend
a long time on the last dense connections without saving useful progress.

The hard-coded local repair helper also contains unchecked trace paths and
orphan-removal operations. Its presence on disk is not evidence that those
operations passed DRC or survived cancellation. Do not invoke either helper
on the working aesthetic board without changing its reset behavior.

run_stage3.py still invokes run_stage2.py, which restores the V1.0 PCB before
rebuilding. These scripts remain unsuitable for resuming local board edits.
Keep the cancelled run and earlier failed candidates as evidence, rather than
using them as a fabrication source.

REMAINING QUALIFICATION / ASSEMBLY ITEMS

The existing back-side 12-32V and 100W text is a design target, not validated
performance. This aesthetic pass did not re-rate the hardware. The earlier
review's unresolved transient coordination, Q1 thermal losses, shunt pickup,
regulator heating, capacitor ripple and motor-current-limit concerns remain.
SMCJ30A's 30V standoff and 48.4V rated clamp do not substantiate a protected
normal 32V input for the 47V-absolute-maximum DRV8701.

The input section is for dry contacts/3.3V logic, not 12/24V sensors. The E-stop
driver disable is distinct from software-read homing/limits; it does not cut
all auxiliary/relay power. Firmware needs safe output-latch-before-direction
initialization, no automatic restart on E-stop release, and tests of MCU reset
while the PCA9555 remains powered.

OLED hole centers remain 23.3mm apart, versus the user's stated 23.0mm pattern;
confirm the exact physical module and header offset before ordering. Confirm
the two-row connector bank with plugged cables, installed StepStick modules,
and mounting hardware. The AUX PTC supplier identity C70125 is still not
independently verified. Existing production BOM/CPL/Gerbers are stale for V1.1
and must not be mixed with the newly saved PCB. An actual gold-colored logo
also depends on the selected fabrication finish, such as ENIG.
