ABX-Motion Rev 1.1b — bounded peak-load assessment, 20 October 2026

User design envelope: up to 150 W per motor for one second as an upper-edge
case. Treat as electrical power drawn in motoring unless explicitly described
as returned braking energy. Both motors simultaneously draw 300 W: nominal
12.5 A at 24 V, 10.20 A at 29.4 V, 9.375 A at 32 V, excluding controller and
conversion losses. Mechanical output watts would require efficiency margin.
This does not by itself bound PWM winding/stall current or repeated heating.

Q1 at 12.5 A and datasheet 18 milliohm cold maximum has about 2.81 W / 2.81 J
for one second. Hot resistance and transient thermal impedance must be
included before making a temperature-rise claim; a one-second peak is not a
continuous rating. No change to the existing current chop is justified just
from this bus-power number.

The pictured D2 area is unchanged. Rev 1.1b was a marking-only pass and no
second TVS has been fitted. The small TVS3300 footprint and solder mask/paste
were verified in the supplier audit. Large masked copper is not an assembly
fault or proof of inadequate electrical connection.

D2 is a transient shunt across the bus. Normal motor power does not flow
through D2. At normal 24–32 V, it is below its 33 V standoff. Comparing the
motor's 150 W / one-second consumption with TVS pulse watts is not a valid
selection method. The old 1500 W SMCJ rating and new 125 W SON rating refer
to specified roughly millisecond surge waveforms, not one second.

If the SAME numerical envelope is separately required for regeneration, the
two motors can return 300 J. Existing four motor-rail bulk capacitors total
nominal 640 uF, storing only 0.235 J extra between 29.4 and 40 V (0.184 J
between 32 and 40 V). They cannot buffer 300 J. A battery/BMS that accepts
the returning energy or an appropriately sized braking path is required.
For scale only, a shared 36 V brake sinking 300 W corresponds to 4.32 ohm
and at least 300 J pulse-energy capability for the one-second example, before
margins, repetition and control tolerances. This is not a selected circuit.
36 V is an illustrative braking level below the drivers' voltage ceiling;
32 V remains the normal operating ceiling. The TVS cannot be treated as a
sustained braking regulator, whether one or two are populated.

Recommendation: assess motoring against the newly bounded 300 W / one-second
total peak; retain short-spike suppression as a separate function. Do not
add an uncoordinated second TVS as a remedy for normal motor consumption.
The prior hardware candidate and manufacturing hold have not been changed
by this calculation-only assessment. No additional motor model is required
to express these bounds. The conservative 300 J regeneration case is an
explicit separate example, not an inference from the user's motoring number.
